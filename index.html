Pioneering Research and Organizational Solutions to Promote Equitable Care through Technology

We are the digital innovation team at Zuckerberg San Francisco General Hospital with the mission of applying technology and digital tools to improve health outcomes and equity in vulnerable and underserved populations. Our team is composed of experts in machine learning, artificial intelligence, data science, informatics, building and analytics within the electronic health record, health equity and social determinants of health. We utilize Lean and implementation science to deploy our technology in healthcare environments serving vulnerable and underserved populations.

For more details, read the ZSFG Predictive Analytics Report.

Heart failure

Standardized inpatient heart failure care with AI and EHR automation. The initiative reduced readmission rates, reduced heart failure mortality rates and closed equity gaps in care between Black/African American patients with heart failure and the general heart failure population.

Chest pain pathway

A decision-support pathway in the emergency department that avoids more than 100 hospital admissions each year, with patients evaluated faster and with fewer downstream tests.

Valve disease

AI identifies severe valve disease and routes patients to treatment. Patients were four times more likely to receive timely specialist care.

Whole Person Navigator

AI that helps social workers review patient records more efficiently, summarizing complex medical and social information so care teams can spend more time with patients and less time searching through charts.

December 2025

Our research exploring how AI could help social workers review patient records more efficiently was presented at the NeurIPS GenAI4Health workshop and accepted for oral presentation at AAAI, one of the leading artificial intelligence research conferences. The work investigates methods for summarizing complex medical and social information so care teams could spend more time with patients and less time searching through charts. Congrats to the team, and a big thank you to the ZSFG social work staff who helped shape this project. Read the paper here.

December 2025

Congrats to the team on having its work on building compact, transparent AI models for healthcare accepted at NeurIPS, a top venue for machine learning research. The method, BC-LLM, learns fully-interpretable risk prediction models that clinicians can use at the bedside, by learning concepts that are commonly mentioned in unstructured clinical notes.
