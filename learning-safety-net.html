A Learning Safety Net for all of San Francisco

Connecting the city's programs and community partners into a central digital platform, so that help is easier to find, referrals are completed, and what works can be seen.

The problem

A generous safety net, fragmented.

San Francisco spends well over a billion dollars each year helping its most vulnerable residents. That money flows through nine city agencies to hundreds of separate service providers. A person in crisis may navigate up to 30 of them to find housing, food, treatment, transportation, or work.

Yet there is no government-maintained compendium of resources, and nobody, not the city, not its philanthropists, not the providers themselves, maintains consistently accessible data about the outcomes of these programs. What cannot be seen cannot be accessed, and what cannot be measured cannot be improved.

The proposal

Three steps to a safety net that learns.

1

Build the network of resources

With ShelterTech, the volunteer-powered San Francisco nonprofit that already maintains a community-built directory of the city's services, we will create a single, continuously updated, verified compendium of every program: what it offers, whom it serves, and whether it has availability.

2

Connect people to help that works

A mobile app accessible to all San Franciscans and a web-based platform for the health care system, clinics, community centers, and senior centers. The platform communicates in natural language to suggest matches to the most appropriate resources, then submits the referral and completes any appropriate forms or administrative tasks.

3

Follow every referral to its outcome

The Learning Safety Net records what happens after each referral and action, following up with both residents and service organizations. Funders gain quantitative evidence for their decisions, gaps in the safety net become visible, and proposed programs can be evaluated before they are funded.

Team

Built on systems already running at ZSFG.

Our team is co-led by UCSF/ZSFG and ShelterTech. The core technology is already built and running at Zuckerberg San Francisco General Hospital, the city's safety-net hospital, where UCSF physicians, data scientists, and AI developers work with the hospital staff to deploy AI-based tools in clinical workflows. Our dedicated social needs platform reads structured and unstructured data to identify social needs, precisely matches patients to resources, and simulates the effect of proposed interventions on patient outcomes.

ShelterTech already maintains a compendium of resources for San Franciscans in need and is in regular contact with community organizations. Their team brings experience in developing and maintaining connections with community-based organizations, and expertise in deploying technologies at a citywide scale.
